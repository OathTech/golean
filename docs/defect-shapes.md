How the classification was made: each entry was read and its shape named in
a few words from its own text and its site; the names were then normalized
into the vocabulary of §1. The plan's §3 seed vocabulary was the starting
point; the reading moved it: «representation leaks into identity» and «a
value crosses a conversion without its effect» stay (S4, S2) but the identity
KEYS the wire mints are a shape of their own (S3); «an absorbing default» is
the core's own shape (S26), distinct from the tool boundaries (S24, retired
from this record 2026-10-27);
«evaluation order» split into three shapes with different remedies (S6 the
assignment phases, S7 spec-ordered events the hoist reorders, S8
spec-unsequenced pairs realized as one member) plus evaluation COUNT (S9);
«zero values, nil-ness and aliasing» split between nil TYPING at a sink (S1),
nil-ness and identity (S4) and binding (S13); the frontend's lowering split
between the emitter's missing arms (S17), the spellings it admits (S28), its
synthetic-symbol bookkeeping (S29) and the admission boundary (S20); byte
transparency of a string-carrying channel is one shape at the wire and the
readout (S27); and the apparatus between the readout (S22), the tool
boundaries (S24, since 2026-10-27 the apparatus record's) and the resource
budgets (S23).

## 1. The shapes (the unit of step 2)

Member counts are over the 175 rows of §2. The `S<n>` ids are what the
`- Neighbourhood:` lines in `docs/BUGS.md` cite.

| id | shape | definition | members |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Implicit conversion omitted at an assignable context | A value flows into an interface-typed or nilable slot — assignment, argument, return, literal element, range target, comparison operand, comma-ok target, variadic spread — without the conversion Go performs there: no box, or an untyped nil left raw. The consumer then compares raw against boxed (a wrong value) or refuses. | 8 |
| S2 | Conversions: a missing kernel arm or a value not retagged | A conversion or assignability the kernel has no arm for (pointer, slice, map, func, array and string/rune targets; nil at an interface or func type; the slice→array length panic), or a struct value or field that crosses a legal conversion keeping the mint tag a consumer then rejects. | 9 |
| S3 | Type identity keys: fusion or spurious distinction | The wire's identity key for a type or a member loses a component Go's identity carries — the import path, the variadic bit, an unexported method's package, `uintptr` — so two types fuse; or it mints a name Go's instantiation does not have (local types, gc's compiler counter). | 8 |
| S4 | Identity, equality and nil-ness | Where Go compares locations, values or nil-ness the model compared a representation or held one member of a latitude: a `Loc` path's static typeId, an allocation's freshness, a zero-size variable's address, a blank field, an empty slice where Go has nil. | 7 (BUG-176 added by the sweep, 2026-10-26) |
| S5 | Method sets: records, promotion, receiver adaptation, dispatch | Satisfaction or dispatch answered from an incomplete record (an absent declaration, promotion missing from the set, a last-writer-wins registration), or the receiver adapted wrongly (auto-deref, address-of) on the call path. | 7 |
| S6 | Assignment and return: the two-phase order | spec#Assignment_statements' two phases — operands and right-hand sides, then the stores left to right with each target's own check at its store — collapsed or inverted in one statement form: the receive statement, select clauses, comma-ok forms, blanks, address chains, single assignment, the multi-value return. | 11 |
| S7 | Ordered events reordered by the hoist | A spec-ORDERED pair (calls, receives, binary logical operations; a `len`/`cap` read before a later call) came out in the other order because the A-normal-form hoist lifted one of them to a statement front, or placed a hoisted call outside the scope or branch it belongs to. | 7 |
| S8 | Spec-unsequenced order realized as one member | Two events the spec does not order — a panicking operand against a sibling call's panic or effect, a target's operand against the right-hand call, a conversion's read against a mutating call — where the model realized ONE order and gc the other; the remedy is an envelope (the `unseq` graph) or a pin to gc's realization. | 11 |
