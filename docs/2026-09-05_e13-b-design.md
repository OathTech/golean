## 1. The decision procedure: unsequenced vs forced, over the AST

The spec sentence (spec#Order_of_evaluation, pinned go1.26.5 text):
"when evaluating the operands of an expression, assignment, or return
statement, all function calls, method calls, receive operations, and
binary logical operations are evaluated in lexical left-to-right order.
... the order of those events compared to the evaluation and indexing of
x and the evaluation of y and z is not specified, except as required
lexically. For instance, g cannot be called before its arguments are
evaluated."

Definitions, over one statement's evaluation phase (the frontend's
"sweep": the statement in `emitStmtList`, or a sub-accumulator — if/for
init·cond·post, switch tag, case values, range operand, short-circuit
RHS, per-spec var initializer):

- **Ordered events O** — the spec's list: function calls, method calls,
  receive operations, binary logical operations, and the built-ins the
  spec says are "called like any other function" (spec#Built-in_functions:
  `len`/`cap`/`min`/`max`/`append`/`copy`/`make`/`new`; BUG-062 is the
  record that `len`/`min`/`max` ARE ordered). In the lowering these are
  exactly the constructs that HOIST to a statement ahead of the residual
  expression (calls, receives, `min`/`max` when an event follows them,
  `len`/`cap` when an event follows them, `append`/`copy`/`make`/`new`,
  and — structurally, not by the spec's list — composite-literal
  allocations and method values).
- **Panicky non-call operands P** — operand syntax that stays INLINE in
  the residual and can raise a run-time panic on its own: an index
  expression on an array/slice/string, or a map read whose key TYPE
  contains an interface (hash panic; `containsInterface`); a slice
  expression; a single-value type assertion; a pointer dereference,
  explicit (`*p`) or implicit (a selector that indirects, go/types
  `Selections[…].Indirect()`, or whose base is a pointer); integer
  division/remainder with a non-constant divisor; a shift by a
  non-constant SIGNED count; `==`/`!=` on an interface-containing operand
  type; a slice-to-array(-pointer) conversion. (This is the census the
  retired A6 guard `sweepPanickyInlineBeforeKinds` mirrored; the
  emitter's `probeKind` is now its ONE home.)
- **subtree(o)** — the argument list / operand expressions of the event
  o (for a call: its arguments and the callee expression; for a receive:
  the channel operand; for `&&`/`||`: its right operand).

**FORCED (class (c) — the machine holds the point; a wrong order here
is a bug, BUG-062's territory):**

- (F1) o₁, o₂ ∈ O: lexical order. Realized by the hoist order
  (statements are emitted in lexical order of the events).
- (F2) p ∈ subtree(o): p before o — "g cannot be called before its
  arguments are evaluated". Realized because the hoisted statement for
  o carries its operands: `wit(t[k])` evaluates `t[k]` at the call's
  hoisted position (probe `v-indexLeftCallArgPanics` in
  `docs/evidence/2026-09-05_e13-b/gc-realization.txt`: gc raises the
  ARGUMENT's panic first; the machine agrees and this design leaves it
  untouched — no probe is ever emitted for an operand of the event
  being hoisted, see §4 rule D2).
- (F3) p in the LEFT operand of `&&`/`||` vs events in its RIGHT operand:
  the left operand decides whether the right is evaluated at all
  (spec#Logical_operators: "the right operand is evaluated
  conditionally") — forced. Realized by the E3 short-circuit
  normalization (`emitBinary`).
- (F4) o ∈ subtree(p) (`a[f()]`): data dependence — f before the index.
- (F5) A receive statement's targets and the assignment's TWO PHASES
  (spec#Assignment_statements; E5 FORCED, L-016) — untouched.

**UNSEQUENCED (the latitude this lane envelopes):** p ∈ P and o ∈ O with
p ∉ subtree(o) and o ∉ subtree(p) and neither (F3) — i.e. SIBLINGS in the
operand set: operands of the same expression, elements of the same RHS
or return list, arguments of the same call, elements of the same
composite literal, the channel operand of a send vs a call in its value.
The spec orders neither pair, whether p is lexically left or right of
o. Two panicky non-call operands p₁, p₂ are ALSO unsequenced against each
other by the same omission (E12(ii)); this lane does not open that axis
deliberately (§6) but its mechanism may realize members of it (§3, "what
the set contains").

**OUT OF SCOPE by construction (other inventory rows, unchanged):**
assignment TARGET operands (`x[j()]`, `aa[i][j] = …` — E2/E3/E4: the
phase-1 target-vs-call and target-vs-target axes, (b)-pinned with their
own re-envelope obligations); the VALUE observable of an unsequenced
pair (E12: `a[i] + f()` where f mutates `i` and no panic fires — the
(b) call-first pin stands, §6); method-call receiver vs arguments (E14).

panicked BEFORE every sibling event):

| operand kind (left of a sibling event) | gc's member | probes |
|---|---|---|
| type assertion | EARLY — panics before the sibling calls/receive/method call/append/copy/make/new/min (w=0) | a, e (middle: w=1 — the earlier call ran, the later did not), g, j, k, l, n, r, s, w, w2, y, z, z2 |
| slice expression | EARLY (w=0) | p |
| interface comparison | EARLY (w=0) | p3 |
| index expression | LATE — after every sibling call (w=5; middle: w=33, both calls ran) | b, f, i, j2, k2, m |
| nil dereference | LATE (w=5) | c |
| integer division | LATE (w=5) | d |
| shift | LATE (w=5) | p2 |
| slice→array conversion | LATE (w=5) | p4 |

And the forced positions, for contrast: `s[i] + wit(t[k])` / `*p +
wit(t[k])` raise the ARGUMENT's panic (v, x) — (F2), the machine's
present behaviour, unchanged. `wit(5) + iv.(int)` (u): the assertion
RIGHT of the call panics after the call — lexical order (§6 residual).
`t[j] + fix()` where `fix` repairs `j` (o): a VALUE, no panic — E12's
call-first pin, unchanged.

Every gc realization above is ONE of the two members the machine now
offers per probed operand (§3): EARLY = slot 1, LATE = slot 0. The
two-axis split E13 recorded (assertion vs index) is therefore not a
machine defect on either axis — it is gc's compiler-internal rule
(`cmd/compile/internal/walk/order.go`: `ODOTTYPE`, `OSLICE*` and
interface `OEQ` are "safe-expression"-ordered early; `OINDEX`/`ODEREF`/
`ODIV`/`OLSH` stay in the residual) landing on two different conforming
members. The upper-bound argument is the spec's silence (I-2 UNSEQ); no
implementation is known to realize a third pattern (XIMPL: none).

## 3. The machine reifies the order as a choice — `Stmt.unseqProbe` + `ChoiceSite.unseqPanic`

Doctrine: no semantic choice hides in evaluator recursion. The
frontend's ANF hoist is where the sibling order used to be BAKED IN
(calls first, residual last); the fix makes the order a machine pick.

**The construct.** A new GoCore statement `Stmt.unseqProbe (e : Expr)`
("evaluate the unsequenced operand `e` at THIS position — or not yet"),
with the continuation frame `Cont.probeK (k : Cont)` and the choice site
`ChoiceSite.unseqPanic`:

- `exec (.unseqProbe e) env k  →  evalE e env (.probeK k)`.
- `retV v (.probeK k)  →  next k` — the value is discarded; the operand
  is re-evaluated at its residual position (it is PURE: calls, receives
  and allocations were hoisted out of it, so re-evaluation is
  observation-free — §3 "purity" below).
- `panicking chain (.probeK k)` — the operand's evaluation PANICKED here:
  the site is consulted at bound 2 (`Choices.consumeAt .unseqPanic 2`):
  slot 0 = **DEFER**: `next k` — the panic is not raised here; the
  operand's residual re-evaluation (after the sibling events) raises
  whatever it raises then (the pre-change machine's ONLY member; gc's
  member for index/deref/division/shift/conversion); slot 1 = **RAISE**:
  `panicking chain k` — the panic propagates now, before the sibling
  events (gc's member for type assertions, slice expressions, interface
  comparisons). `canonicalSlot0` = DEFER, so the empty/default stream
  reproduces the pre-change trajectory exactly (the strict lane's
  default runs are byte-identical; no baseline moves on the default
  trajectory).
- `Cont.probeK` is its own `FrameClass` (`.probe`): NOT glue —
  `panicPassthrough` does not strip it (the `.panicking` arm acts on
  it), `recoverTransparent` does not cross it, `break`/`continue`/
  `return` have no rule at it (unreachable: only expression evaluation
  happens under a probe; fail closed if ever reached).

**The bound is data-dependent and the consult is on the panic path
only.** A probe whose operand evaluates to a value consults NOTHING (no
`consumeAt` call at all — `retV` simply continues); a probe whose
operand panics consults at bound 2. Consequence, and the reason for
this shape rather than a "consult at the probe statement" pick: a
corpus row acquires a pop ONLY when an unsequenced operand actually
panics before its sibling events — exactly the E13 observable. Rows
where nothing panics early (the ordinary `a[i] + f(x)` idiom, `x[i]`
beside a method call in raft) keep their stream untouched: no
re-indexing of existing rows' streams, no `wideAfterExhaustion`
movement, no depth declarations (§7).

**The consumption projection (B8).** `seqConsumption` gains ONE arm:
`.panicking _ (.probeK _) ↦ some (.unseqPanic, 2)`. Its FORM is unchanged
(a function of the pre-configuration to site × bound); the theorems
`stepFn_consumption_none/some` gain the corresponding case; the pool
projection `poolConsumption` funnels to it unchanged; the dedup checker's
oblivious fragment (`poolThreadOblivious`, `innerVecs`) excludes the new
consuming shape fail-closed via a `consumesUnseqPanic` flag beside the
existing five (`stepFn_oblivious`'s hypotheses grow by one). The census
mirror (latitude inventory §0) gains its row; `ChoiceTrace.siteName`/
`allSites`/`seqFacts` gain the site (spec width: exactly 2 at a
`.panicking _ (.probeK _)` configuration, nothing anywhere else).

**The relation (merge invariant).** `Step` gains four constructors
(FIX ROUND: "three rules" was a miscount, audit R12): `unseqProbe`
(exec → evalE under `probeK`), `probeValue` (retV → next), and the
two-successor pair `probeDefer`/`probeRaise` at `.panicking chain
(.probeK k)` — nondeterminism where Go has it. The reachability
invariant that makes these the ONLY rules at a `probeK` frame (no
`.breaking`/`.continuing`/`.returning` can arrive there: only expression
evaluation runs under a probe) is written at the rules' site
(Machine.lean). `stepFn` names the unreachable travellers through its
existing `.internal` catch-all: the fix round did NOT add explicit
`.probeK` arms — every added arm shifts `MachineSound`'s positional
case tags (`case183`/`case184`/`case186` at three theorems broke on
the attempt, a nested match inside the catch-all broke them too), and
reworking the verified core's proofs is outside this round ([AGENT]).
`stepFn_sound` covers the new arms (slot 0 → `probeDefer`, slot 1 →
`probeRaise`). No `sorry`, no `partial` in the core.

**Purity of the probed operand — the soundness argument, stated once
(FIX ROUND: restated per audit R7/R8).** The realized observable set of a
probe is a SUBSET of the two total orders' observables: the operand `e`
contains no call, receive or hoisted allocation (hoisted out by
construction — its residual references temps), no `recover()` (the
frontend never probes an operand containing one, and the DECODER refuses
a probe whose expression contains `.recoverCall`, fail closed against
frontend drift), and no ALLOCATING CONVERSION — `[]byte(s)` /
`[]rune(s)` are the two inline operands whose evaluation allocates
(`applyStrictOp`'s `.bytesFromString`/`.runesFromString` call
`s.alloc`), so a probe over them would allocate twice and advance the
`Loc` counter before the residual; the first cut's "changes no state"
was FALSE for exactly these two (no observable escape found — addresses
are not observables — but the claim is repaired by a refusal, not a
caveat): the frontend never probes such an operand
(`containsAllocatingConversion`) and the decoder refuses a probe whose
expression carries either wire head (`jsonMentionsAllocatingConversion`;
closed enumeration of two). With those three exclusions, evaluating `e`
early changes no state and emits no observable; if it panics early, the
RAISE member is exactly the "operand-first" total order's observable
(same state, same texts), and the DEFER member is exactly the pre-change
"events-first" trajectory. A probe never absorbs a refusal: `stuck`/
`unsupported`/`internal`/`fatal` are `throw`n `Stop`s that never reach a
`.panicking` configuration, so slot 0 can only defer a RECOVERABLE
run-time panic. RACE DETECTION is an OVER-approximation, not
idempotence: under DEFER the operand's memory is read TWICE in program
order (the probe, then the residual), so a write that races the first
read but happens-before the second yields a race report the residual-
only trajectory would not — a fail-closed over-report (a racy program
refused, never a missed race), recorded as §6 item 8; the footprint of
the consult step itself is empty.

**What the set contains, exactly.** For a sweep with hoisted events
e₁…eₙ (lexical) and probed operands p₁…pₘ, each pⱼ that has at least one
event lexically after it is independently at its lexical position (RAISE)
or in the residual (DEFER). With one probed operand and any number of
events that is precisely the ruling's "BOTH orders" (the operand before
all later events, or after all of them). With two probed operands both
panicking early, the DEFER×RAISE combination realizes p₂'s panic ahead
of p₁'s — a member the spec ALSO permits (non-call vs non-call order is
unspecified by omission, E12(ii); I-2 UNSEQ) but wider than E12(ii)'s
left-to-right pin. Recorded on E12/E13 as a by-product widening on
panic-vs-panic shapes only (it changes no value observable: DEFER re-
evaluates in lexical residual order); the weakest machine admits it by
right and this design does not build machinery to suppress it.

## 4. The frontend: where probes are emitted, and the guard retirement

All in `tools/nativefrontend/emit.go`, the hoist/guard surface only:

- **D1 — one hook.** `emitExpr` (the wrapper around `emitExprBare`)
  pushes the accumulator length on a node-start stack, emits the node,
  then: if the node is of a `probeKind` (the §1 census, ONE function,
  reused nowhere else — the retired guard's census dies with it) and
  probing is not suppressed (D4), it pops trailing probe entries appended
  since the node's start (a child's probe is subsumed by its parent's:
  `x[a[i]]` probes once, for the whole operand) and appends
  `{"stmt":"unseq-probe","expr": <the node's wire>}` to the accumulator
  — at the operand's lexical position among the hoists, because hoists
  are appended in emission = lexical order.
- **D2 — forced positions never get a probe.** Every hoisting construct
  appends through `pushHoist`, which first drops the trailing probes
  appended since the CURRENT node's start: an operand of the event being
  hoisted is evaluated by that event's statement (F2), so its probe would
  be a wasted pop at best. `f(a[i], g())` keeps `probe(a[i])` (it sits
  before `$g`, a sibling event); `f(a[i])` drops it. The short-circuit
  wrapper (`$c := lhs; if $c {…}`) is such an append: the LHS operands'
  probes are dropped (F3).
- **D3 — trailing probes are pruned** wherever an accumulator is
  captured (`takeHoists`): a probe with no event after it in its sweep
  is order-transparent (the residual evaluates the same operand next,
  nothing effectful between) and would only ever cost a pop.
- **D4 — what is probed beyond plain operands (RE-AUDIT, rewritten;
  the FIX ROUND text "assignment targets are not probed" was WRONG and
  a regression — R1'-1).** spec#Assignment_statements phase 1: "the operands of
  index expressions and pointer indirections ... on the left and the
  expressions on the right are all evaluated in the usual order" — a
  target's index operand is a SIBLING of the RHS's calls and, being a
  non-call, UNSEQUENCED against them (gc raises `x[iv.(int)] = wit(5)`'s
  assertion FIRST, no output). Suppressing its probe pinned the
  events-first member: `m[iv.(string)] = wit(5)` had two members with
  gc's in the set at b2fd9f15 and ONE without at d75049c0. So: (i) the
  PHASE-1 target paths — the `=` statement's targets
  (`emitAssignTargetPhase1`), the single map-assign store's base and
  key, the compound/IncDec read-write target
  (`emitReadWriteTargetPhase1`; the read `x[k]` is probed as a whole,
  it is phase-1 material of `x = x op y`), the receive-statement form's
  targets (`x[k] = <-ch`; the statement RECEIVES before it evaluates
  its targets, so its trailing probes are KEPT — `eventBeforeResidual`)
  — emit their operands through the hook; the target's OWN index/deref/
  map-write check is the phase-2 STORE's and is neither probed nor
  counted by the census (`walkUnprobedPanicky` exempts a `=` target
  node, judges its operands). `probeSuppress` stays ONLY where the spec
  FORCES the target after its event: a `select` RecvStmt's left-hand
  side (spec#Select_statements step 4), a `range` clause's targets, the
  comma-ok forms (no event). (ii) An ADDRESS-OF operand is a probeKind
  node itself: `&a[i]` is a bounds-checking `index-addr`
  (`indexTargetLoc`), `&p.f` through a pointer a nil-checking
  `field-addr` (measured: `sink(&q.Data)` with q nil panics before sink
  prints, gc and machine alike), `&*p` the nil-asserting
  `addr-of-deref` — all pure. (iii) An ARRAY-of-array target base
  (`aa[i][j]`'s `aa[i]`, an `index-addr` that never reaches the hook) is
  probed at `emitAddressOf`. (iv) `recover()` is an ordered event the
  emitter already HOISTS (`$c := recover()`), so an operand spelled
  `recover().(int)` has the pure residual `$c.(int)`: the exclusion is
  decided on the emitted WIRE (`wireMentionsExpr`), not the syntax, and
  such operands are probed; the decoder's `.recoverCall` refusal stays
  the backstop. (v) An allocating conversion (`[]byte(s)`/`[]rune(s)`)
  HOISTS to a temp when an ordered event follows it in the sweep
  (`allocatingConversion`; the allocation is unobservable, the
  conversion cannot panic), so the enclosing operand's residual
  (`$b[1:7][0]`) is pure and probed; only an INLINE conversion (no
  event after, a forbidden hoist, or a PANICKY operand — hoisting would
  drop the operand's probe, so `[]byte(s[i:j])` stays inline and the
  operand's probe realizes both orders of ITS panic) still excludes the
  enclosing operand.
  WHATEVER D4 LEAVES UNPROBED IS THE BOUNDARY D5 REFUSES AT — after the
  re-audit that residue is: a compound/IncDec target that CONTAINS A
  CALL (its address is a hoisted temp, `emitReadWriteTarget`'s other
  path — BUG-104's territory), a method-call RECEIVER operand (E14's
  axis, `receiverAddr`), a forced (select/range) target, an inline
  allocating conversion's operand.
- **D5 — the A6 unordered-panic guard is RETIRED for probed material
  and REINSTATED, NARROWED, for unprobed material (FIX ROUND, audit
  R1).** The first cut deleted `hoistReordersPanic`,
  `residualPanicFreeOperand`, `nilDerefOnlyResidual`,
  `sweepPanickyInlineBeforeKinds` and the `emitMake`/`len`/`cap` refusals
  whole: the composition they refused (a panicky hoisted operand beside
  panicky inline material to its left) is a two-member set the probe
  realizes — `iv.(int) + len(b[j]) + f()` lowers to `probe(iv.(int)); $l
  := len(b[j]); $f := f(); return iv.(int) + $l + $f`, whose members are
  gc's interface conversion (RAISE) and the index panic (DEFER). But the
  deleted guard had swept the WHOLE statement, so on the material D4
  does not probe the composition went from a visible refusal to a silent
  single-member answer ≠ gc: `x[iv.(int)] = len(b[j]) + wit(5)` (gc the
  conversion, the machine `index out of range [5]`), `x[iv.(int)] +=
  …`, `m[iv.(string)] = …`, `x[iv.(int)] = len(make([]int, t[k]))`, `r =
  recover().(int) + len(b[j]) + wit(5)` in a defer, `[]byte(s)[7] +
  len(b[j]) + wit(5)`. The guard is reinstated on exactly that residue:
  `hoistReordersUnprobedPanic` = `residualPanicFreeOperand` (verbatim
  from b77f3298) × `unprobedPanickyBefore` (the old sweep census MINUS
  every node in `probedNodes` — the set of nodes the hook appended a
  probe for, dropped later or not: a dropped probe means a forced
  position covered by the event that evaluates it) with FR-28's
  `nilDerefOnlyResidual` transparency (verbatim: a nil-deref-only
  target left of a nil-deref-only hoisted operand — raftpb's
  `out.Data = make([]byte, len(x.Data))` — is one runtime error either
  way; the target's indirection is in fact a phase-2 store check).
  Note that `probeKind` also NARROWS the old census: float division, a
  constant divisor and an unsigned shift count are not panicky and are
  neither probed nor refused (the retired sweep counted them).
  Wired at the len/cap hoist (with the event-after condition) and in
  `emitMake` over every size/hint operand (unconditional, as the hoist
  is) — RE-AUDIT (R1'-2): and at the `min`/`max` hoist (when an event
  follows) and the unconditional `append`/`copy` hoists, with
  `residualPanicFreeOperand`/`nilDerefOnlyResidual` recursing into
  `min`/`max` like `len`/`cap` (the first fix round answered "a real
  call, residual is a temp" for them while `runtimeOrderedCall` keeps
  them inline — the guard short-circuited, `x[iv.(int)] =
  len(make([]int, min(q, t[k])))`-class); refuses BY NAME (`… hoisted
  past UNPROBED panicky material to its left (<kind> at <file:line:col>)
  …`, naming the E13 (b) boundary; `lowerdiag` cause
  `len-hoist-panic-order`, live again). RE-AUDIT (R1'-3): the census
  `walkUnprobedPanicky` prunes a hoisted call's subtree only when the
  call lies wholly LEFT of the hoisting construct (`call.End() <=
  before`: its operands are forced at its own statement); a call that
